# Fix the casting loop before anything else

The biggest usability gain comes from making a correct cast work and explain itself, because a user can currently finish every documented step and still get no spell, with no message saying why. The live trace reaches the classifier as a whole frame squeezed to 28×28, without the cropping and centring the training drawings effectively had. In simulation, accuracy fell from 99% to about 44% as the gesture shrank, and small gestures were almost always called "close". The app requires "open" first and stays quiet on a wrong-state cast, so the likely first experience is a working wand and nothing happening. The docs also describe the wrong gestures, the camera settings users are told to tune are not applied by the main app, and any stray movement that reaches the classifier can fire a spell. These fixes are small, mostly hours rather than days, and should come before the web UI, smart-home integration or a flashable image. After them, the highest-value work is a three-question setup with a real go/no-go check, wand-captured spells defined in data, and headless operation. **Nothing here was tested on a Pi, camera or wand**: the findings come from code reading, simulation on a Mac and external sources, so about half a day on real hardware should come first and may reorder the list. I assumed "usable" covers the first-time builder, the person casting spells, and the tinkerer adding spells; the notes contain no observed user data for this project.

## Three compounding defects make a correct install look broken

The failure comes from three defects interacting. First, training images are drawn to fill a 300×300 canvas ([draw_spell_data.py](../DatasetCreation/draw_spell_data.py#L28)), while the live path blurs, thresholds and resizes the entire 640×480 frame ([harry_potter_wand_cv.py](../harry_potter_wand_cv.py#L252)). The `cv2.dilate(mask, (3, 3))` call also passes a tuple rather than a kernel, so it barely dilates. Re-rendering held-out samples at different sizes and positions shows the effect ([simulation results](../research_notes/Wand%20project%20usability%20improvements/runtime_and_spell_workflow_audit.md)):

| Trace size, position | Current pipeline accuracy | Share predicted "open" | Retrained on normalised traces |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 450 px, centre | 99.0% | 55% | 100% |
| 300 px, centre | 68.3% | 49% | 100% |
| 200 px, centre | 44.6% | 1% | 100% |
| 120 px, centre | 43.6% | 0% | 100% |

The 100% column is optimistic, because these are synthetic re-renderings of mouse drawings with no wand jitter, dropouts or lens distortion. It shows that size and position sensitivity can be removed, and it does not forecast real accuracy. The likely real-world size is the worrying part. With the documented 120° wide camera at 1–2 m, a 60 cm arm movement spans an estimated **70–140 px**, inside the band where the model answers "close" nearly every time ([audit inference](../research_notes/Wand%20project%20usability%20improvements/runtime_and_spell_workflow_audit.md)).

Second, the [state lock](../harry_potter_wand_cv.py#L261) fires "open" only when `lastMove == 0` and "close" only when `lastMove == 1`. A fresh session that gets "close" therefore prints `Prediction: 1` and nothing else. Third, a successful cast is barely visible: [`screen_flash_feedback` has no callers](../harry_potter_wand_cv.py#L220), [LEDs ship disabled](../config.yaml#L13), and the window status only ever says "Ready..." or "Tracing...". The README's "draw more deliberately" advice cannot help with any of this.

The instructions make it worse. The [README lists spell names but no shapes](../README.md#L147), and the [Technical Guide says "upward swirl" and "downward swirl"](../docs/TECHNICAL_GUIDE.md#L786). The shipped samples actually show a circle with a vertical stroke through it (open) and a square hook like a "P" or flag (close) ([rendered samples](../research_notes/Wand%20project%20usability%20improvements/setup_onboarding_audit.md)). Separately, the main app [sets only size and format](../harry_potter_wand_cv.py#L53), so it most likely runs on auto exposure, while the [calibrator hardcodes 8000 µs, gain 6.0 and brightness -0.3](../calibrate_reflector.py#L174). The wizard's and docs' exposure advice therefore does nothing at runtime, and thresholds tuned in the calibrator are tuned under different exposure.

The repair is mostly small. Add one `preprocess_trace(points)` used by both training and runtime that crops to the bounding box, pads to a square and re-renders from the point list at a fixed stroke, then retrain. Show a gesture card rendered from the shipped data, labelled "as seen on screen" because the [frame is mirrored](../harry_potter_wand_cv.py#L283). Send prediction events to the main loop through a queue, so the window can show the spell name, the 28×28 input and "already open: cast Colloportus" without repeating the GUI freeze that commit 7e21647 fixed. Create one shared `open_camera(config)` for the app and calibrator, passing `ExposureTime`, `AnalogueGain`, `FrameDurationLimits`, manual focus and `LensPosition` at configure time, when they [apply from the first frame](https://pip.raspberrypi.com/documents/RP-008156-DS-picamera2-manual.pdf). Expect this to change detection behaviour and require retuning, and expect LED and reflector wands to need separate presets.

The same pass should remove the verified state-machine bugs. In simulation, a first LED-mode trace where the wand vanishes immediately [crashes on a `None` frame](../harry_potter_wand_cv.py#L358), and on later casts the same path classifies the previous trace. The "too short" cancel is unreachable because still frames add points. Stillness is [counted in frames against an assumed 20 fps](../harry_potter_wand_cv.py#L186), so the documented 1.0 s hold lasted about 0.7 s at 30 fps and 1.4 s at 15 fps in simulation ([results](../research_notes/Wand%20project%20usability%20improvements/runtime_and_spell_workflow_audit.md)). Normalising also makes a small flick look like a full gesture, so minimum path length, bounding box and duration gates must ship with it.

## Rejection needs a new recogniser path, not just a threshold

Today every trace reaching the classifier becomes a spell: [the code returns `clf.predict` directly](../harry_potter_wand_sklearn.py#L35), 300 of 300 random scribbles were classified, and an X-shaped trace fired Alohamora in simulation. A margin threshold on the current SVM is a weak fix. Held-out samples had decision margins of 0.44/0.84/1.22 at the 5th/50th/95th percentiles against 0.04/0.54/74 for scribbles, so the distributions overlap heavily ([experiment](../research_notes/Wand%20project%20usability%20improvements/runtime_and_spell_workflow_audit.md)). Accuracy on deliberate gestures also says little about false triggers. A Kinect whole-body recogniser with 99% test accuracy fell to 83% once a thresholding background model was added and still produced a false positive every 5.1 s per participant in natural activity, though a wand that must be aimed and lit should fare much better than whole-body tracking ([Freeman et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.06109)).

The two audits differ on the next step. One proposes keeping the SVM and adding a trained "none" class; the other proposes moving to an ordered-path template matcher. **I recommend normalising now, then spiking a template matcher on logged wand traces, and adding a "none" class only if the SVM wins that comparison.** Templates fix three problems at once: they give a natural distance score for rejection, need no retraining step, and keep stroke direction, which images discard. The published evidence is strong but comes from a different modality. On stylus input, $1 error fell from 2.73% with one template to 0.45% with nine ([Wobbrock et al.](http://faculty.washington.edu/wobbrock/pubs/uist-07.01.pdf)), and an independent test scored it 95.56% on the same dataset, so setup matters ([Squiggle paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.5323)). Protractor matched nine samples across 16 gestures in 24 ms on a 2008-era phone ([Li](https://research.google.com/pubs/archive/36269.pdf)), so compute should not be the limit on a Pi 5, though no Pi benchmark exists. Default rotation invariance should stay off because it would collapse orientation-dependent spells. Air traces have no pen-up and more tremor, so none of these numbers transfer directly. The shipped PNGs also lack stroke order, so stock templates would have to be re-recorded with a wand. That is my inference and the main cost of switching.

A small CNN and pulsed-illuminator differencing are the two upgrades most likely to be assumed necessary, and neither looks like the current limit. [Inference is cheap on a Pi 5](https://github.com/aallan/benchmarking-ml-on-the-edge), but data is scarce. Differencing applies only to reflector mode because a handheld LED has no link to the camera, needs the [illuminator on for a whole rolling-shutter frame](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10949647), and halves the effective tracking rate.

Trigger timing deserves the same evidence-first treatment. The audit's simulation resolved a question the technique notes left open: in reflector mode, MOG2 absorbs a held wand, so a "hold still" actually ends through the wand-left-frame path about 1.3 s after stopping, and about 0.77 s of the gesture's start was lost to the restarted presence timer ([results](../research_notes/Wand%20project%20usability%20improvements/runtime_and_spell_workflow_audit.md)). Use real timestamps, define "still" as staying within a radius of about 15 px, keep a ring buffer of pre-start points, and add a maximum trace time. A pause-then-move start is worth testing, since a pause prefix cut swipe false positives 15.2× in the [Kinect study](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1509.06109). The hold should also shrink from 1 s, because stylus gestures themselves take only [0.7–1.8 s](http://faculty.washington.edu/wobbrock/pubs/uist-07.01.pdf). A 0.3–0.5 s hold is a starting point to tune on logged traces, not a published value.

Failure feedback should name the cause: "too small", "lost tracking", "not recognised, closest was X". The strongest evidence is from mouse input, where feedforward guides cut unaided error from 7% to 4% ([OctoPocus](https://www.lri.fr/~mbl/FundHCI/papers/Octopocus-UIST2008.pdf)). A comparable project's user report of [a tracked wand whose shapes simply do not trigger](https://github.com/mamacker/pi_to_potter/issues/3) shows the same silent failure elsewhere. Log raw `(x, y, t)`, outcome and exposure for every attempt, because every later comparison depends on that data. A reasonable acceptance test is at least 97% recognition on a fixed logged set (a threshold [Arif et al.](https://www.asarif.com/pub/Arif_CHI2014b.pdf) cite secondhand) plus misfires per hour of idle waving.

## Setup needs three decisions, not 25 questions

The README's scripts all exist and chain together, and the suspected missing `train_spell_classifier.py` is a false alarm. The problem is that the path around them misleads. Since `config.yaml` ships, [pressing Enter at the wizard's first prompt cancels setup](../setup_wizard.py#L242). A full run asks 25 questions, 7 of which have no effect on the main app, yet it [never asks whether LEDs exist](../utils/config_builder.py#L77). The generated config then reads as LEDs-on in [the main script](../harry_potter_wand_cv.py#L90) and LEDs-off in [the demo](../test_led_demo.py#L33). Rewriting the file also erases comments and calibrated values ([run results](../research_notes/Wand%20project%20usability%20improvements/setup_onboarding_audit.md)). Only wand type, LEDs yes/no and LED count need a human decision. Everything else can be defaulted or detected, and non-functional keys should be implemented or removed. A tracked `config.defaults.yaml` plus an untracked overrides file would stop the wizard and calibrator destroying each other's work and avoid `git pull` conflicts.

The installer carries the largest unverified risk. It [installs apt OpenCV and NumPy](../install.sh#L61), then [pip-installs unpinned `numpy` and `opencv-python`](../install.sh#L87) with `--break-system-packages`. Current `opencv-python` [requires NumPy 2 or newer](https://pypi.org/pypi/opencv-python/json), and users report [picamera2 failing with "numpy.dtype size changed"](https://forum.sunfounder.com/t/valueerror-numpy-dtype-size-changed-may-indicate-binary-incompatibility-expected-96-from-c-header-got-88-from-pyobject/2899) in that situation. It may not reproduce on Trixie, but [`test_setup.py` would not see it](../test_setup.py#L20) because it calls `find_spec` without importing. The fix follows platform guidance whichever way the test goes: [apt for picamera2](https://github.com/raspberrypi/picamera2), and a [venv with `--system-site-packages`](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/computers/os.html) plus a pinned `requirements.txt` for the rest. Pinning also addresses model compatibility, because scikit-learn calls cross-version loading ["entirely unsupported and inadvisable"](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/model_persistence.html). Training from the shipped dataset during install avoids shipping a version-bound model; this took seconds to about half a minute on a Mac, and Pi time is unmeasured. Another comparable project shows the cost of neglect: [its apt package list stopped working on Bookworm](https://github.com/mamacker/pi_to_potter/issues/7).

Failures need to surface earlier and more honestly. A missing model appears only as a [thread traceback at first cast](../harry_potter_wand_cv.py#L258), although `install.sh` calls training optional. [`mixer.init()` runs unguarded](../harry_potter_wand_cv.py#L37), so a missing audio device should abort startup, yet neither speaker nor monitor is in the parts list, and the docs recommend SSH despite [unconditional `imshow`](../harry_potter_wand_cv.py#L376). The [reboot check looks only at group membership](../install.sh#L213), not at whether SPI was just enabled, and [`./install.sh --help`](../install.sh#L237) runs the full install, including `apt-get upgrade -y`. Add a startup preflight that reuses the existing graceful LED and servo pattern. Add a `make doctor` that truly imports modules, captures a frame, respects optional hardware and prints one fix per failure, since a camera-only user can never get "all passed" today. Wand Spell's first screen, where ["only the wand tip should be white"](https://github.com/Mootado/wand-spell), is the right model for a pass/fail tracking step, and it answers the [daylight question another project left unanswered](https://github.com/sean-obrien/rpotter/issues/5). Its author's lamp-placement rule, which [Wand Portal shares](https://github.com/mtaylor45/-SpellSight), belongs in first-run docs, and it should be checked on this hardware.

Calibration should move from knobs to measurement. The tool exposes [10 manual values](../calibrate_reflector.py#L63), and a [`last_position` bug](../calibrate_reflector.py#L288) rejected 40 of 40 frames after a far-away reappearance in simulation, inviting users to tune thresholds for a problem thresholds cannot fix. It also runs a different detection pipeline from runtime. Make it call `ReflectorDetector` directly, then guide the user: capture about 3 s of empty scene to mask static IR sources, then about 5 s of waving to measure tip brightness and size, derive the thresholds, and warn if the room has too much IR. LED mode needs this most, since a static bright spot blocked or corrupted tracing there in simulation, while reflector mode's MOG2 handled the same lamp correctly. No study supports this routine directly, so treat it as a design to test.

A `--source picamera|webcam:N|file.mp4` flag, `--no-audio` and the existing [`neo=None` path](../utils/animations.py#L65) would let people try the pipeline before buying parts, an [off-Pi request seen elsewhere](https://github.com/mamacker/pi_to_potter/issues/4). A bundled clip would also become the trace-to-prediction regression test the project lacks. A documentation sweep should accompany this, including removing every code line-number reference and extending `tests/test_documentation.py` to check anchors and referenced scripts; `make test-local` currently passes and declares the code "ready for deployment" despite all of the above.

## Spells as data and headless operation turn a demo into an appliance

The README's custom-spell promise is false and harmful. It says ["The new spell will be recognized"](../README.md#L224), but the [converter labels anything not containing "open" as class 1](../DatasetCreation/convert_to_training_data.py#L32), so new samples silently merge into Colloportus, and [`counter = 0`](../DatasetCreation/draw_spell_data.py#L33) overwrites existing files on each run. A real new spell needs 7–8 edits across 4 files, plus an mp3 and 50–100 mouse-drawn samples ([edit map](../research_notes/Wand%20project%20usability%20improvements/runtime_and_spell_workflow_audit.md)). Correct the README immediately, before any code changes. The durable fix is a `spells.yaml` registry with per-spell folders and a capture tool that uses the real detector, with prompts, auto-save, undo and a warning when a new spell is too close to an existing one. Capturing with the wand removes the train/live mismatch at its source and, with a template recogniser, could cut enrolment to about 3–10 examples. [Wand Portal claims about 5 samples per spell](https://github.com/mtaylor45/-SpellSight), with a reliable limit of 6–10 spells, but that is one author's untested claim on a weeks-old project, so borrow the design and verify the numbers.

The same registry should hold effects, which today are an [`if/elif` in the prediction thread](../harry_potter_wand_cv.py#L261). A cast should produce one event, and a dispatcher should run its listed actions off the detection thread with timeouts and a cooldown, so a dead bulb cannot stall recognition. For reach per line of code, start with MQTT using Home Assistant's [stateless `event` platform](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/event.mqtt/), since Home Assistant then covers bulbs and everything else. Then add a generic webhook and a one-call [WLED preset](https://kno.wled.ge/interfaces/json-api/). Avoid a plugin system, and note that demand for any of this is assumed: no project-specific user data exists.

Appliance operation follows from there. The script has [no CLI flags, logging or service](../harry_potter_wand_cv.py#L376), and a SIGTERM would likely skip cleanup and could leave LEDs lit. Add `--headless`, journald logging, a systemd unit with restart and a signal handler, then a `wand.local` hostname and a small FastAPI or Flask page, the pattern used by [Wand Portal](https://github.com/mtaylor45/-SpellSight), [WandGestureDetector](https://github.com/DanStach/WandGestureDetector) and, for known local addresses, [Home Assistant](https://www.home-assistant.io/installation/raspberrypi). The design rule is to protect the detection loop: stream on-demand MJPEG from one shared latest-frame buffer, preferably the threshold mask and trail, encoded only while someone is watching. WebRTC looks excessive for a tuning preview because the [Pi 5 has no hardware H.264 encoder](https://pip-assets.raspberrypi.com/categories/685-app-notes-guides-whitepapers/documents/RP-010033-WP-1-H.264%20encoding%20performance%20on%20Raspberry%20Pi%205_series%20computers.pdf), though no Pi 5 streaming benchmark was found, so measure loop FPS with zero, one and three viewers. A settings-changing UI [should not be left open by default](https://docs.frigate.video/frigate/installation/). Defer a flashable image, captive-portal Wi-Fi and OTA, since they add a release pipeline with little benefit until the installer is stable. Avoid Docker for the production path because [CSI passthrough is fiddly](https://github.com/kbingham/libcamera/issues/35). Keep [Pi5Neo over SPI](https://github.com/vanshksingh/Pi5Neo) behind a small interface, avoid [rpi_ws281x on Pi 5](https://github.com/jgarff/rpi_ws281x/issues/528) unless its status has improved, and move the servo off [pigpio, which a collaborator says does not support Pi 5](https://github.com/joan2937/pigpio/issues/589), onto gpiozero's lgpio factory, subject to testing.

## Half a day on hardware should set the order of everything else

A clean-image Pi 5 session settles most open questions at once: installer behaviour and the NumPy conflict, reboot and SPI defaults, startup without audio or a display, real frame rate, auto-exposure behaviour, real trace sizes at 1 m and 2 m, and stock accuracy with a wand. Have one new person attempt setup from the README and time it, because all severity rankings here reflect code evidence and judgement rather than measured drop-off. The effort figures below are my rough roll-ups of the notes' per-item estimates, and none of them is measured.

| Order | Work | Effort | What it unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Hardware baseline; correct README spell and gesture text; add raw attempt logging | Half to 1 day | Evidence for everything below |
| 1 | Crash and state bugs; shared normalisation with quality gates; fixed camera controls; outcome overlay and gesture card | 1–3 days | A correct cast works and says what happened |
| 2 | Pinned venv install; correct reboot flow; preflight; `make doctor`; three-question wizard; config overlay | 2–4 days | A first spell without code reading |
| 3 | Time-based trigger; template spike against SVM; rejection with specific failure messages | 2–4 days | Fewer misfires, and a decision on the recogniser |
| 4 | Wand capture tool; `spells.yaml`; action dispatcher; MQTT and webhook | 3–5 days | Custom spells and smart-home use |
| 5 | Guided calibration; `--source` modes; headless service; web UI | 1–2 weeks | Screen-free use and phone tuning |

Licensing is a separate track that blocks sharing rather than using. This repo has no LICENSE file, its [README defers to the upstream project](../README.md#L291), and [that project has no licence](https://github.com/Gloworm72/InteractiveWand), which generally means [no clear permission to modify or redistribute](https://choosealicense.com/no-permission/). Ask the upstream author for a licence covering code, data and sounds, and audit the provenance of the three files in `Sounds/`, which the notes did not inspect. Selling kits or images would raise the franchise risk, as [crowdfunding takedown precedents](https://torrentfreak.com/?p=165478) suggest. This is not legal advice.

## Conclusion

The project's reputation as a tuning problem looks misdiagnosed. A [task file records unreliable detection and lighting issues](../PRPs/tasks/calibrate-reflector-fine-tuning.md), and the git history shows threshold reverts, but the audits point upstream of the thresholds: unapplied camera settings, a calibrator testing a different pipeline, and a classifier seeing a different image from the one it trained on. Until those are fixed, more knobs will only produce more confusing results, and the published recogniser numbers will not be reachable or even measurable. The prediction to falsify first is simple: at 1–2 m with a real wand, the stock model should answer "close" nearly every time. If it does, recognition is the first job. If it does not, the camera or field-of-view assumption is wrong and the sequence should change.

The newest comparable projects reach the same conclusion from another direction. They compete on setup order, visible rejection and few-sample training rather than classifier accuracy, so 99% offline accuracy does little for this project's users. The durable investment is the raw trace log, because it turns each later choice, including recogniser, hold time and rejection threshold, from an opinion into a replayable test.
